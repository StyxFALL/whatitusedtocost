YT What Channel

The publishing app of the What It Used To Cost YouTube channel — prices measured in hours of work, then, now, and where you live.

Every episode takes an official price series and divides it by the wage, so the unit is time rather than money. That is the one measure that lets you compare 1980 with today, and one country with another.

What gets measured

  • How many hours of work a thing costs — groceries, rent, a car, a doctor, a degree
  • What a fixed amount of money used to buy, and what it buys now
  • Inflation and the value of money — and why those two say the same thing
  • Purchasing power across countries: where is expensive, where is cheap

Where the numbers come from

  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — consumer prices and average hourly earnings
  • FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis) and the U.S. Energy Information Administration
  • Eurostat — prices, price levels, net earnings, spending structure
  • International Labour Organization — hourly and median earnings
  • World Bank, International Monetary Fund, Bank for International Settlements

How the numbers are handled

  • The method is stated in every episode and the series identifiers are in every description
  • Months that were never published are shown as gaps, never filled in
  • Where the answer depends on the starting year, both years are shown
  • Forecasts are cut out; the last actual year is named on screen
  • When a measure cannot be used, the episode says so instead of substituting something else

No forecasts. No advice. No verdicts on policy. No assets recommended.

YT What Channel

YT What Channel is the channel's own publishing application. It uploads finished episodes to the What It Used To Cost YouTube channel as private videos and sets their title, description and thumbnail. It is used only by the channel owner and accesses no other account. See the privacy policy for exactly what it accesses.
